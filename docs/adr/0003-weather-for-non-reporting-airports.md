# Weather for a Non-reporting airport: altimeter from a Substitute station, temperature from a model forecast

A Non-reporting airport (`GLOSSARY.md`) used to leave its temperature and altimeter blank for the pilot to fill (#226). The fetch now fills both automatically: the altimeter as a Substitution with a Caution (ADR 0001), the temperature with an Advisory (see below). The Non-reporting Advisory says what was filled, or why the altimeter wasn't. The two fields deliberately come from different sources:

- **Altimeter from a Substitute station.** This is the nearest airport, by distance only, that reports an altimeter, within 100 nm. An altimeter setting is already reduced to sea level, so it carries over between nearby fields, and 14 CFR 91.121 allows a station within 100 nm. The station's altimeter goes through the same time selection as any reporting airport. If no station is within 100 nm, the field is left blank for the pilot, and the Non-reporting Advisory says why. We rejected the Open-Meteo point forecast's `pressure_msl` for this field. It is reduced to sea level differently from an altimeter setting, and in the mountains it can be off by a few hundredths of an inch. That error goes straight into pressure and density altitude.
- **Temperature from a model point forecast at the field's own position and elevation.** Surface temperature depends on the field. A station's temperature, even after correcting it for the elevation difference, comes from a different valley and adds an extrapolation on top of the substitution. If the forecast fails, the field is left blank with a Warning (Partial fetch). Reporting airports use the same field point forecast whenever their observation and TAF give no temperature for the requested time (#301). Under ADR 0001's updated rule that forecast is data for the field, not a Substitution, so it raises an Advisory, for a Non-reporting airport as for a reporting one (#226). Only the altimeter is a Substitution with a Caution.
- **Terrain is left to the pilot.** We don't filter stations by elevation or ridgelines. Instead, the altimeter Caution names the station with its distance and direction from the field (e.g. "KYYY, 18 nm NE"), so the pilot can judge whether terrain lies between them.

Unknown airports have no coordinates, so they are not covered and the pilot still enters their data by hand.
