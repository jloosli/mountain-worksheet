# Limit checks are separate from notifications, and the worksheet states no verdict

The Mountain Flight Worksheet sets its own mountain-flying limits: mountain winds under 30 kt, climb over 300 fpm along the route, takeoff plus landing ground roll under runway length, ceilings and visibility much better than marginal VFR, and weight under 90% of maximum gross. ADR 0001 classified "beyond a hard limit" as a Warning and "close to a limit" as an Advisory, but those levels classify whether worksheet data can be trusted. A 35 kt wind at 9,000' is valid, trustworthy data that fails a limit. We model it as a separate concept, a **Limit check** (`GLOSSARY.md`):

- **Not a notification.** A Limit check never withholds a value, and it doesn't count as a Warning, Caution, or Advisory. Reusing the Warning level was the obvious alternative, and we rejected it for three reasons. Under ADR 0001 a Warning withholds the values that depend on it, but the pilot still needs the TOLD and climb numbers when winds fail. Red would mean both "this data is broken" and "this data is fine but the conditions fail". The Warning counts on step badges would mix the two.
- **One per worksheet limit**, covering every altitude or airport it applies to. It is in one of four states: Within limit, Near limit (only for limits that authoritative guidance gives a caution threshold), Exceeded, or Not checked (an input it needs is blank or withheld). Its state is the worst among its points, and it names the points that fail.
- **Aircraft limitations and entry ranges stay Warnings.** Weight above maximum gross, or an altimeter outside its entry range, means a result can't be trusted. That is a data-trust problem under ADR 0001, not a Limit check.
- **No verdict.** The worksheet reports each Limit check but never says Go or No-Go. That decision belongs to the pilot in command and the Flight Release Officer, as the printed Briefing already says.
